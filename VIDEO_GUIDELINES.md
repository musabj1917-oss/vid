# Video quality guidelines (reusable for any topic)

Core rule: build the video around ONE visual metaphor that fits the topic. Every scene shows that metaphor changing. The picture must explain what the voiceover says.

Do NOT reuse a flat isometric slab/grid template. Build a real 3D scene: lit materials, real meshes, perspective camera.

## Objects
- Every object is a recognizable real-world thing built from many small parts, never a generic box or slab.
- Break each object into labeled sub-parts, like a product teardown.
- Every scene has moving parts (gears turning, pieces sliding apart, things falling, gauges sweeping). Nothing sits still.
- Show before/after on the same object: the problem state, then the fix applied to it.
- Use scale cues: tiny characters, hands, props.
- The main object fills at least 60% of the frame. No empty areas.

## Camera and pacing
- Cut to a new angle or scene every 2-3 seconds (10+ scenes in 30s).
- Move the camera constantly: wide, push in on a part, pull back, orbit.
- Never hold one static view for more than 3 seconds.
- Hook in the first 2 seconds. End with a clear call to action.

## Look
- Lit 3D with real materials (glass, metal, glow, soft shadows).
- Dark background with one warm accent color. No flat-fill shapes.

## Text
- Captions: 3-5 words at a time, big, bottom-center, spoken word highlighted. No paragraph boxes.
- Callouts are short and point at specific parts of the object (2-4 per object).
- Key numbers are big animated counters or gauges, fully inside their cards.

## Proof
- Include one proof beat: a real screenshot, stat, or source on screen. Text must not overlap or overflow.

## Check before delivering
- Count cuts (need 10+), and confirm each scene has a different angle and moving parts.
- Can you tell what each object is without reading its label?
- Is anything on screen for more than 3 seconds without changing?
- Does any text overflow its card or overlap other text?
